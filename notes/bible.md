## Canon facts (fixed)

- Shalamar is the jungle New World. Landfall is the old continent, the Empire of Vorkathia, a theocracy with an
  Inquisition. Elves are enemies of the Vorkathian state; elven refugees fled the Inquisition.
- "Before the war, before the mushroom, before the walls came down", Klhyts Refuge "was a sweaty, loud, magnificent
  mess of a town". "The nicest Town in Shalamar": colonial architecture, good pubs. A coastal port.
- "The Custodian skimmed the top, Dominico kept the peace with a fist, Fat Gog owned the shadows."
- Six taverns, each with canon prices, clientele and look (LoreKeeper doc "Klhyts Refuge Taverns"): the
  Omberhulk & Nun, the Touchy Unicorn, the Rampant Ogre & Curved Cleaver, the Turtle Dragon, the High Apothecary,
  the Stripperwitch. "Six different Klhyts Refuges happening at the same time, and none of them talking to each other."
- The Stripperwitch is Fat Gog's real seat of power; River, his partner, runs the bar. The Custodian "pretends he
  doesn't visit. He does." Dominico has a reserved table at the High Apothecary; his informants drink at the
  Omberhulk, his off-duty muscle at the Rampant Ogre.
- Fat Gog: "the Marcelles Wallis of colonial fantasy"; "Lizard men and humans living in harmony". He found Klhyt
  starving and thirsty, rescued him, and is a trusted member of the community (owner, 2026-11-22). Read with the
  truncated canon line "Klyht: A Castaway… Was found on…", Gog found him when he washed ashore (host reading).
- Fat Gog is the guild master in this era. Dominico becomes guild master only after he kills Fat Gog (owner,
  2026-11-22): LoreKeeper's "A Guild master and casual dabbler in the magic arts" describes him in that later era.
  Here he is the Custodian's enforcer (owner, 2026-11-22) who "kept the peace with a fist", a dabbler in the magic
  arts, and wants the guild.
- Klhyt: "A Castaway, who founded the first western Shalamar Town." Fat Gog found him starving and thirsty and saved
  his life; Klhyt owes him everything.
- Lizardfolk fishermen drink at the Turtle Dragon "without much trouble, before the tension started rising".
- High elves on the run eat at the High Apothecary; an officer in uniform came after them, and they had already left.
- Named townsfolk: Husok Sourfist (left a woman with twins), the Bard of Moonport, the woman with a large fishbone
  (lemon baths). Hanklevode (human mercenary thief out of Malice) and Thoradin (dwarven ranger from Landfall, seeking
  a druid) are canon people; placing them here is a host default.
- Captain Eirik is a canon Xoton envoy; placing him here is a host default. Xotons: seafaring dwarves, say it with an
  "X"; Xoton traders pass through the Refuge.
- Phillip Everuex is canon, of the Everuex noble family (the River Queen). Deloris and Kael Ironjaw are canon
  characters; placing them here is a host default.

## Hired help: the only way to a second agent (owner, 2026-11-22)

> "I want to make this possible but very limited, you have to go in and hire people in world. And only then can you
> get any sub-agents to your character. Thats the only way to do it." Then: "Not everyone works for money.. or some a
> LOT more money."

The owner's picks: only people the world marks for hire; days paid up front, the seat ending when they run out; a
character keeps 1 + their Charisma modifier in their hire at once (at least one); the rule binds players, and the
narrator still gives the world's own people minds when a scene needs one. A hireling hires nobody, so agents never
chain. Each hire offers the hirer the hireling's seat (`claim_seat` makes the sub-agent's paste); unplayed, the
hireling follows the hirer. Dismissing them, or their death, ends it too.

Who is for hire, and their price, are **host defaults** for the owner to correct (build.py, `HIRELINGS`):

- **Tully**, Dominico's bruiser: 2 sp a day, 3 days at most, and Dominico must not hear of it.
- **Kael Ironjaw**, sellsword: 5 sp a day, up to 7.
- **Hanklevode**, mercenary thief: 2 gp a day, up to 7. Doors, not blood.
- **Deloris**, assassin: hard to win. A favour first (end Phillip Everuex's hunt for her, marked done by the narrator),
  then 25 gp a day, 3 days at most. She joins as herself: her seat is never offered to the hirer (below).
- **Thoradin**, ranger: no coin. A favour first (find him his druid; Saelwyn is one), then three days. The narrator
  marks the favour done with `favoured: {<the hirer's id>: true}` on him.
- **Ssark**, Meters fisherman: no coin. A tot of rum, poured for him first, buys a day.

> ok. I encountered quite a few NPCs that were way way too one dimensional and had no backstory, or didn't engage in
> conversation or didn't move or seem to do anything. Have a look at Kalypso's log? We really need to up our game on
> the personality of our characters and what they are up to ? Give them routines, jobs, their own money, their plans
> or schemes (if they have any) do we need a lot more of them as well? Also River needs to be a secret witch with her
> own pirate vessle down the way (but this is all top secret). Also the narrator tells the users too much. Often
> should describe what people look like rather then outright just say who they aare and what they are going to do

(Asked, the owner picked: nobody in town knows River's secret, not even Fat Gog; and a law and turf network of new
people, each with a job and a routine.)

What Kalypso's log showed (the hosted world, night one): a person with no mind answers `talk` from their table, and
anything off it got one stock line. An elf said "Yes?" to a hello and to a drink; Big Ludo said "Drink." twice; the
chandler answered a question about lodging with his wares; Fat Gog, told Klhyt was free outside, recited the beach
story; Klhyt, told Gog was inside, gave a stock line; nobody moved all night.

What is built:

- **Everyone has a cast entry** (`cast/*.py`, merged by `build_cast`): a `look` (what a stranger sees: no name, no
  title, no intent), `seen_as` (how a stranger would point them out), a `brief` for an agent who may play them (backstory,
  job, money, wants, scheme, secret, fears, voice), a `scheme`, a purse sized to their life, a table of 12 to 20 topics
  with the words players really type, 3 to 5 lines of their own for when nothing fits (said in turn: the engine's
  `default_line` list), a day (`routine`) and small visible deeds (`beats`). The lines, the day, the scheme and the
  beats are on each person's `secret` list: players and their narrators see the look and hear what is said.
- **A name comes in talk, not in the look.** Descriptions are look plus what they are doing now. The story's own scene
  lines describe people the same way (scripts/story.py `seen`), and a name comes when someone says it. The briefing
  tells the narrator the same (`SHOW_BRIEF`: people are seen before they are named; never say what someone is about
  to do, wants or hides; one or two telling details at a time).
- **The town's day** (`scripts/routine.py`, a `routine_tick` on the root every 5 world minutes): each slot moves a
  person's post (d20's idle walk takes them there), sets what they are doing into their description, hands over its
  pay, and tells the room they leave if a player is in it. While a player is in a room, one person at work there shows
  it each tick. A day waits for a held body, a hireling, a follower, someone tied, down or dead, someone the story just
  walked (`routine_hold_until`, half an hour), someone the story is done with (`routine_off`: the elves aboard, Klhyt
  shipped), and Klhyt and Brask until the story frees them (`routine_when`).
- **Money moves.** Purses are sized to each life. Sabeth Crane collects Dominico's peace money from the Ogre, the
  Turtle and the Omberhulk on her round; Dominico pays his men at 2; Marlo pays Gog his cut; regulars pay for rounds.
- **The night's news reaches people as flags** their lines answer to (`if_state`): on everyone `klhyt_home`,
  `klhyt_shipped`, `guild_hit`; on Fat Gog `gog_knows`; on Dominico `backed_off`; on the Custodian `disgraced`; on
  Klhyt `bound`; on Brask `left_post`; on the elf household `aboard`. The story no longer rewrites anyone's lines.
- **The Back Lanes**, behind the square, where most of the town sleeps.

### The faith (LoreKeeper)

From "History of Vorkath" (3be19d14), "Vorkathia" (aaba6499), "THE GODS summary v002" (e081bb5b) and "KARGATH -
format" (aeda85df, the older draft of the setting, where the goddess is Kargath; read Vorkath). The full digest with
sources is kept beside this build's notes as `notes/vorkath.md`.

- **Vorkath** is a goddess, the newest of the New Gods: an eight-armed woman holding a saint's sign in each hand.
  Greater Vorkathia (Aurelia, Öchland, Fronteria joined in 300EV) is her theocratic empire under Emperor Arkmady
  Melenkov, whose missions go "to the known and unknown corners of the globe". The sign is the **octogram**.
- **The Eight Arms** are her aspects, each with its patron saint and sign: War (Gideon the Everliving, ball and
  chain), Peace (Percival the Builder, spade), Pleasure (Pil's Loffgren, One For The Road, wine and stout), Pain
  (Teflon, First of Many, founder of the Inquisition, hourglass and spider), Chaos (Captain-Lady Hester-Stanhope, The
  Fearless, who discovered Shalamar, storm and ship), Destiny (Annabella Rand, the Blind Savant, sextant and glass
  eye), Death (Gwendoline, Handholder to Heaven, a child's hand and sweets), Life (Vistadine the Baptist, the pouring
  jug). The Shalamar world names only Gideon and Teflon; the other six are the Kargath draft's.
- **The Inquisition**, founded at Kirchheim by Teflon, purges "the impure": the elven races are enemies of the
  state; unsanctioned magic and other gods are suppressed; penance runs from death to the Aurelian mines.
- **Host defaults** (the lore leaves them open): the library burning at Kirchheim (the lore has Kirchheim as the
  Inquisition's seat, and the Hall of Osmith burned in 240); how each Arm judges; colours (none given).
